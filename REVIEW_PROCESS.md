# Candidate → Bank promotion process

Five stages. An entry only advances when its stage's checklist is genuinely
satisfied — not stamped. This document exists because the bank currently has
49 approved entries whose own `review.notes` field still contains an
unresolved question addressed to a reviewer (see `BANK.md`, generated by
`tools/build-bank.mjs`). This process is designed to stop that from
recurring, not just to describe an ideal pipeline.

---

## Stage 0 — Candidate intake (`CANDIDATES.md`)

A raw idea enters here. Minimum bar to log a row:

- [ ] Both sides sourced for **existence and meaning** — a dictionary
      (RAE/Cervantes for Spanish; Treccani/De Mauro for Italian), a corpus, or
      a native speaker's own material (see note below on reviewer-sourced
      material).
- [ ] Checked against the existing bank, alternatives, and candidates with
      `tools/lookup.mjs` — no silent duplicates.
- [ ] A one-line note on why the pairing might work, or what's uncertain
      about it.

**On reviewer-sourced material:** when an allowlisted native reviewer
supplies the expression themselves (as with the 2026-07-27 Italian batch),
that upgrades confidence on *their* side's currency — a native speaker
choosing to teach something is stronger evidence than a dictionary entry.
It is **not** equivalent to that reviewer having reviewed the pairing itself.
Log the provenance explicitly and still route the finished draft through
Stage 3 like any other entry.

---

## Stage 1 — Drafting (`bank/NNNN.json`, `status: needs_review`)

The candidate becomes a full entry: bridge note, interference in both
directions, examples, register, regions, themes, CEFR, difficulty.

- [ ] `node tools/validate.mjs` passes with no errors (warnings for
      unverified sources are expected and correct at this stage).
- [ ] Every open judgment call the drafter could not resolve alone is written
      into `review.notes` as an explicit, answerable question — not buried in
      prose, not left implicit.
- [ ] `example` / `exampleTranslation` on both sides are original sentences,
      written for this entry — never copied or lightly adapted from a cited
      dictionary, corpus, or reviewer's own teaching material. Sources are
      cited for meaning and existence; they are never a source for the
      example sentence itself. (Caught and fixed retroactively in 0112,
      2026-07-27 — the Italian example had been Treccani's own illustrative
      sentence. Check this on any older entry before assuming it's clean.)
- [ ] **Regional claims stay non-specific unless a named native reviewer signs them.**
      Where an expression varies across the Spanish-speaking world, say that it varies and
      what the competing readings are — do not map one reading to one country. Usage is not
      uniform within a country either, and it shifts by register, generation and context, so
      a country-to-meaning table reads as more authoritative than anyone can actually be.
      "Unreliable across borders" is honest; "means X in Colombia" is a claim needing a
      signature. A reviewer may of course add the specific version, and then their id
      carries it. (Applied in 0258, qué lástima / che peccato, 2026-08-01.)

- [ ] `esReviewer` / `itReviewer` / `reviewedOn` are left `null`. Nothing
      writes into these fields at this stage under any circumstance.

---

## Stage 2 — Source verification (`verifiedBy` / `verifiedOn`)

Someone with the `verifier` role actually opens each cited URL and confirms
it says what the entry claims.

- [ ] Each source in `es.sources[]` and `it.sources[]` has `verifiedOn` and
      `verifiedBy` filled by a person who opened the link — not inferred from
      a search snippet.
- [ ] `node tools/validate.mjs` shows zero "not yet verified" warnings for
      this entry.

This stage does **not** require native fluency — it requires someone to
click the link and check. `jf` holds `verifier` for exactly this reason.

---

## Stage 3 — Native review (the actual gate)

This is the stage the naturalness principle is about, and the only one that
requires `esReviewer` / `itReviewer` to be a genuinely native, allowlisted
person for that language.

- [ ] The reviewer receives the entry through
      `node tools/build-review-lists.mjs --simple` — **blocked right now**:
      the `--simple` flag is currently a no-op (declared, never read, in
      `tools/build-review-lists.mjs`). Until it's fixed, every "simple"
      worklist still contains the full bridge note, interference claims, and
      drafting notes — including, in at least one case, language that signals
      which answer the drafter expects. Fix this before running Stage 3 at
      any volume; a review that can see the hoped-for answer isn't
      independent.
- [ ] Every question logged in `review.notes` during Stage 1 gets an actual
      answer from the reviewer, written back into the entry — not left in
      place and stamped over.
- [ ] The reviewer confirms or corrects: meaning, register, formality,
      regions, and the specific interference claims (does the trap really
      trip a learner, or was it invented in the drafting pass).
- [ ] `esReviewer` and `itReviewer` are set to the reviewer's own allowlisted
      id, by the reviewer's own action or with their explicit, entry-specific
      confirmation — never inferred from a general "I approved this
      elsewhere" statement with no record of what was actually checked.

---

## Stage 4 — Promotion (`status: approved`)

- [ ] `reviewedOn` set to the actual date of review.
- [ ] `review.notes` rewritten to reflect resolution, not left with dangling
      questions. This is checked mechanically: `tools/build-bank.mjs` flags
      (⚑) any `approved` entry whose notes still match
      `reviewer to (confirm|add|drop|check|verify)`. An entry with that flag
      is not actually done, regardless of its status field.
- [ ] `node tools/build-bank.mjs` regenerated so `BANK.md` reflects the
      change.

---

## Batching

Reviewers reviewing one phrase at a time doesn't scale — batch by
`node tools/build-review-lists.mjs`, which already groups outstanding
entries per language. Stage 0–1 (sourcing and drafting) can run continuously
and far ahead of Stage 3; the bank already has more drafted than reviewed,
which is fine. What isn't fine is drafts crossing into `approved` without
Stage 3 actually happening.

## Auditing

Run `node tools/build-bank.mjs` any time and read the ⚑ section at the top
of `BANK.md`. That count should trend toward zero as real review happens. If
it's rising, entries are being promoted faster than they're being reviewed.

---

## Shipping-field hygiene

`bridge.note`, `bridge.interference.*`, `cue`, and both sides' `meaning`, `example` and
`exampleTranslation` are **learner-facing**. They appear on cards, in emails and on the
site. They must never contain:

- **Pointers to other cards.** No "see also", "compare X", "the sibling phrase Y",
  "as in the entry about Z". A subscriber sees ONE card, in isolation, on a day they
  did not choose. They cannot look up the card being referenced, may never receive it,
  and may have seen it four months ago. A cross-reference is meaningless to them and
  implies a reading order the product does not have. This applies to naming the other
  expression as well as to citing its id — replacing "see 0017" with "see non chiudere
  occhio" does not fix it. Keep the linguistic point and rephrase it to stand alone:
  instead of "the same ne as in averne fin sopra i capelli", write "that ne is one of
  Italian's hardest-working particles". Audited 2026-08-14: 14 entries were pointing at
  other cards, several introduced the same day while removing bare ids. Re-run before
  any push:

  ```
  node -e "const fs=require('fs');
  const RE=/\b(see also|compare |cross-?referen|the sibling|neighbouring pair|banked at|another entry)/i;
  for(const f of fs.readdirSync('bank').filter(x=>x.endsWith('.json'))){
    const e=JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync('bank/'+f));
    for(const v of [e.bridge.note,e.bridge.interference.es_to_it,e.bridge.interference.it_to_es])
      if(v&&RE.test(v)) console.log(e.id);}"
  ```

  Genuine cross-references — scheduling collisions, saturation warnings, themed threads —
  belong in `review.notes`, which never ships and may use ids freely.

- **Bank entry ids.** A subscriber has no idea what 0017 is. Cross-references are welcome
  and often valuable — name the expression instead ("compare *estar en las nubes*"), never
  the number. Audited 2026-08-01: 19 entries were leaking ids into bridge or interference
  copy, some dating from the original authoring pass and five introduced the same day.
  Re-run the check before any push:

  ```
  node -e "const fs=require('fs');const re=/\b0[0-9]{3}\b/;
  for(const f of fs.readdirSync('bank').filter(x=>x.endsWith('.json'))){
    const e=JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync('bank/'+f));
    for(const v of [e.bridge.note,e.bridge.interference.es_to_it,e.bridge.interference.it_to_es,e.cue])
      if(v&&re.test(v)) console.log(e.id);}"
  ```

- Reviewer names or ids, "PROPOSED", "reviewer to confirm", "TBC", or any other instruction
  aimed at the team rather than the reader.
- Process vocabulary — "banked", "queued", "this entry", "the bank" — which describes the
  product's machinery rather than the language.

Everything internal belongs in `review.notes`, which never ships. When a cross-reference is
genuinely for the scheduler rather than the reader (keep these two apart, run them as a
pair), it goes in `review.notes` and may use ids freely.
