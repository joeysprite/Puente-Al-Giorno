Español

O sea

“or be-it (subjunctive of ser)”

That is; I mean — the conversational rephraser.

Llega el viernes, o sea, pasado mañana.

She arrives Friday — that is, the day after tomorrow.

Italiano

Cioè

“that is (ciò + è)”

That is; I mean — the conversational rephraser.

Arriva venerdì, cioè dopodomani.

She arrives Friday — that is, the day after tomorrow.

El puente · Il ponte

Unguessable in either direction, and you'll use it constantly: both languages build their rephraser on 'to be' — o sea is a subjunctive of ser, cioè is ciò+è ('that is') — but nothing on the surface tells you they correspond.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

The calque 'o sia' lands on a real Italian word — ossia — but the wrong one: ossia is formal, written 'or rather'. The conversational filler is cioè.

IT → ES

The calque 'eso es' means 'exactly!', not 'I mean'. The rephraser is o sea (conversational) or es decir (formal).
