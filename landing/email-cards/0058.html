Español

Entre marido y mujer, nadie se debe meter

“between husband and wife, nobody should get involved”

Stay out of other people's marriages.

No opines sobre su pelea: entre marido y mujer, nadie se debe meter.

Don't weigh in on their fight — never come between a couple.

Italiano

Tra moglie e marito non mettere il dito

“between wife and husband do not put your finger”

Do not interfere in a couple's business.

Lascia perdere: tra moglie e marito non mettere il dito.

Leave it alone — don't come between a couple.

El puente · Il ponte

A rare double: both proverbs rhyme, and both rhyme on the act of meddling — Spanish pairs mujer with meter, Italian pairs marito with dito. Spanish states the rule impersonally, as law. Italian makes it physical and second-person: keep your finger out. And the two languages order the couple in opposite directions, husband first in Spanish, wife first in Italian.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

The Italian is an imperative aimed at you, not a general principle — and the fixed order is moglie before marito.

IT → ES

The Spanish has no finger and no imperative; it legislates rather than warns.
