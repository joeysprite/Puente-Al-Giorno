Español

Imagínate

“imagine-yourself”

Just imagine; wow — an intensifier of surprise or empathy, NOT a reply to thanks.

—Esperamos tres horas. —¡Imagínate!

“We waited three hours.” “Can you imagine!”

Italiano

Figurati

“figure-yourself”

Don't mention it; not at all; also 'of course not' — the standard wave-off of thanks.

—Grazie mille! —Figurati!

“Thanks so much!” “Don't mention it!”

El puente · Il ponte

A false friend built from the same instruction: both tell you to picture something. Italian's figurati has drifted into courtesy — picture how little trouble it was — and become the standard reply to thanks. Spanish imagínate stayed an amazement marker: picture how big it was. Same imperative, opposite directions: one shrinks the favour, the other inflates the story.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Don't bring imagínate into courtesy; Italian figurati shrinks the favor. Answering 'gracias' with 'imagínate' confuses a Mexican — the wave-off is 'no hay de qué' or 'de nada'. Save imagínate for astonishment.

IT → ES

Don't bring figurati into amazement; Spanish imagínate inflates the story. Replying to grazie with a literal translation fails; figurati (or figurati!) is the ritual. And when an Italian says figurati to decline your apology, no imagining is requested.
