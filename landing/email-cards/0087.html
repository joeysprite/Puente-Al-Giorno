Español

Caérsele a uno las alas del corazón

“for the wings of one's heart to fall off”

To lose heart; to become discouraged.

Tras seis meses buscando trabajo se le cayeron las alas del corazón.

After six months job-hunting he lost heart.

Italiano

Cadere le braccia

“for one's arms to fall”

To be dismayed; to lose all impetus.

Dopo sei mesi di ricerche gli sono cadute le braccia.

After six months of searching his arms just dropped.

El puente · Il ponte

Discouragement as a body part giving way, and the choice of part is the whole difference. Spanish drops the WINGS OF THE HEART — an organ that had been flying and now cannot, romantic and slightly literary. Italian drops the ARMS — the plain machinery of doing things, which simply stops. One language loses its lift, the other loses its grip.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Wings and hearts are not the Italian image; there the arms fall — and note it takes essere: gli SONO cadute le braccia.

IT → ES

'Caérsele los brazos' is understood but is not the fixed Spanish phrase; the heart's wings do the falling.
