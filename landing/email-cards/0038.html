Español

A ojo de buen cubero

“by the eye of a good cooper”

By eyeball estimate; roughly, without measuring.

—¿Cuánta sal le pongo? —A ojo de buen cubero.

“How much salt do I add?” “Just eyeball it.”

Italiano

A occhio e croce

“by eye and cross”

Roughly; at a guess; approximately.

A occhio e croce ci vorranno due ore.

At a rough guess it'll take two hours.

El puente · Il ponte

Both languages measure by eye, and both own the bare form — a ojo, a occhio — but their elaborations part company beautifully: Spanish summons a professional, the cooper whose trained eye sized barrels without a gauge, while Italian adds a cross from the loom. Weavers who lost their warp threads had to pick them back up by eye, held crossed against the weft — no measuring, hence the phrase, which traces to Latin ad oculum. A craftsman versus a craft, guarding the same guess.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Calquing the cooper — 'all'occhio del buon bottaio' — is nonsense in Italian. In the kitchen say a occhio; for estimates, a occhio e croce.

IT → ES

'A ojo y cruz' is not Spanish. Bare 'a ojo' carries it everywhere; a ojo de buen cubero adds the flavor.
