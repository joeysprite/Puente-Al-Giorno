Español

Faltarle a uno un tornillo

“for someone to be missing a screw”

To be a bit crazy; to have something not quite right upstairs.

¿Renunciar sin otro trabajo? Le falta un tornillo.

Quit with no other job? He's got a screw loose.

Italiano

Mancare un venerdì

“to be missing a Friday”

To be a bit crazy; to be not all there.

Ha lasciato tutto ed è partita, le manca proprio un venerdì.

She dropped everything and left — she's clearly a bit crazy.

El puente · Il ponte

Madness as a missing piece — but of what? Spanish removes hardware: a screw gone from the machine of the mind, something mechanical come loose. Italian removes a DAY: gli manca un venerdì, he's short a Friday — from Robinson Crusoe's Friday, or from the idea that a person missing a day of the week has lost part of their very calendar. A machine with a loose part versus a week with a hole in it.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Screws are not the Italian image; the madman is short a weekday — gli manca un venerdì. (Also common: gli manca una rotella, a little wheel.)

IT → ES

'Le falta un viernes' means nothing in Spanish; the missing part is a screw — un tornillo.
