“the last drop that makes the glass overflow”

The final small thing that exhausts one's patience.

That call was the last straw.

Italiano

La goccia che fa traboccare il vaso

“the drop that makes the vase overflow”

The final small thing that exhausts one's patience.

Quella telefonata è stata la goccia che ha fatto traboccare il vaso.

That call was the last straw.

El puente · Il ponte

Almost the same sentence in both languages, differing by one vessel. Spanish overflows a COPA — a drinking glass, something at the table, personal and about to be spilled. Italian overflows a VASO — a vase or jar, larger, standing somewhere in the house. English abandons liquid entirely for a camel and a straw. Two Romance languages agree on the physics and quibble only about the container.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

The Italian container is the vaso, not the copa — and Italian usually drops 'last': la goccia, not l'ultima goccia.

IT → ES

'El vaso' in Spanish is a drinking glass too, but the fixed phrase says copa and keeps última.
