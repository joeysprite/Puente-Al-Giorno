Español

De buenas a primeras

“from goods to firsts”

Right off the bat; suddenly and without warning, with no time to prepare.

Estaba muy tranquila y, de buenas a primeras, se enojó y se fue.

She was completely calm and then, out of nowhere, got angry and left.

Italiano

Su due piedi

“on two feet”

On the spot; immediately, without time to think it over.

Non so risponderti su due piedi, fammici pensare.

I can't answer you on the spot — let me think about it.

El puente · Il ponte

One idiom still shows its picture; the other has worn smooth. Italian puts you on your two feet — you are standing, nobody has offered you a chair, answer now. Spanish says de buenas a primeras, a phrase whose literal reading (from goods to firsts) means nothing to anyone, including Spaniards: the image eroded centuries ago and only the function survived. Learners can reason their way into the Italian and simply have to memorise the Spanish.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

There is no Italian version of the Spanish phrase to calque — and note the fixed number: su DUE piedi, never 'su un piede'.
