B2

Español

Estar hasta las narices

“to be up to the nostrils”

To be fed up; to have had enough.

Estoy hasta las narices de sus excusas.

I'm fed up with his excuses.

Italiano

Averne fin sopra i capelli

“to have it up over the hair”

To be completely fed up.

Ne ho fin sopra i capelli di questa situazione.

I've had it up to here with this situation.

El puente · Il ponte

Both measure exasperation by how high it has risen — Spanish to the nose, Italian above the hair. A classic Romance mismatch: same meaning, different verb and different body part.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Spanish builds this with estar, so Spanish speakers produce 'essere fin sopra i capelli'. Italian needs avere — and the ne, which Spanish has no equivalent for and drops almost automatically.

IT → ES

Italian's avere pulls speakers toward 'tengo hasta las narices'. Spanish needs estar: estoy hasta las narices. Tener only works in the causative — 'me tienes hasta las narices', you've got me fed up. Spanish also doesn't reach for hair here; the measure is the nose.
