Español

Ser cuatro gatos

“to be four cats”

To be hardly anyone; a very thin crowd.

Fuimos al estreno y éramos cuatro gatos.

We went to the opening and there was hardly a soul.

Italiano

Essere quattro gatti

“to be four cats”

There to be hardly anyone; a very thin crowd.

Siamo andati alla prima ed eravamo quattro gatti.

We went to the opening and there was hardly a soul.

El puente · Il ponte

The freakiest twin in the bank: same animal, same number, same shrug. Both languages independently settled on FOUR CATS as the unit of a disappointing turnout — not three, not a handful, and not dogs. When two languages agree this precisely the interesting question flips: not what diverges, but whether one borrowed it from the other, or whether four cats is simply what an empty room looks like around the Mediterranean.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

This one transfers whole — but mind the frame: Italian builds it with esserci (eravamo quattro gatti), not with avere.

IT → ES

Equally safe coming back. Spanish uses ser or haber: éramos cuatro gatos, había cuatro gatos.
