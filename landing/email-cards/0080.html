Español

Tengo calor

“I have heat”

I'm hot (temperature).

Tengo calor, abre la ventana.

I'm hot, open the window.

Italiano

Ho caldo

“I have hot”

I'm hot (temperature).

Ho caldo, apri la finestra.

I'm hot, open the window.

El puente · Il ponte

Both languages use HAVE, not BE — and both punish the learner who reaches for BE in exactly the same way. Estoy caliente and sono caldo are each heard as a statement of arousal, not temperature. The parallel is your friend here; do not break it.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

An English-influenced Spanish speaker who has learned to avoid 'estoy caliente' sometimes still produces 'sono caldo' in Italian. Same trap, same fix: ho caldo.

IT → ES

Mirrored exactly: 'estoy caliente' is the same error as 'sono caldo'. Use tengo calor.
