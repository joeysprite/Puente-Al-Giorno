Español

¡Sepa!

“may-one-know!”

Who knows; beats me — the one-word shrug.

—¿A qué hora llega? —¡Sepa!

“What time does he arrive?” “Who knows!”

Italiano

Boh

“(bare shrug syllable)”

Dunno; who knows — the minimal Italian shrug, felt as untranslatable.

—Dove sono le chiavi? —Boh.

“Where are the keys?” “Dunno.”

El puente · Il ponte

The minimal shrug, built oppositely: Italian compresses ignorance into a bare syllable that is not even a verb — boh — while Mexico compresses it into pure grammar: sepa, a lone subjunctive ('may one know') with the knower deleted. One language shrugs with sound, the other with mood. Both are one word, both are complete answers, and both are too casual for your boss.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

There is no verb inside boh to translate — reaching for 'non so' is correct but loses the shrug. Boh is learned by ear, not by rule.

IT → ES

Boh means nothing in Spanish. The Mexican monosyllable is ¡sepa! — and note it is subjunctive, a grammar joke your textbook never made.
