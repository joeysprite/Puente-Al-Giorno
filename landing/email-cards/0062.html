Español

No hay mal que por bien no venga

“there is no bad that does not come for good”

Every misfortune brings something good with it.

Perdí el vuelo, pero ahí conocí a mi socia: no hay mal que por bien no venga.

I missed my flight, but that's where I met my business partner — every cloud has a silver lining.

Italiano

Non tutti i mali vengono per nuocere

“Not all the bad things come in order to harm”

Some misfortunes turn out to be for the best.

Ho perso il treno e ho evitato lo sciopero: non tutti i mali vengono per nuocere.

I missed the train and dodged the strike — some bad turns work out.

El puente · Il ponte

The same consolation offered at two different strengths. Spanish makes a universal promise — there is NO misfortune that fails to bring some good, no exceptions, said to someone who has just lost something. Italian only concedes a share: not ALL misfortunes are there to harm you, which quietly admits that some are. One language guarantees the silver lining; the other allows it. Both are built on a negation, and neither survives being translated word for word.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

The calque 'non c'è male che non venga per bene' is not the Italian proverb, and it overpromises — Italian claims some, not all.

IT → ES

'No todos los males vienen para dañar' is not a refrán; Spanish states it absolutely, with the double negative doing the work.
