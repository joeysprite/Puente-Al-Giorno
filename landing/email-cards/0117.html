Español

Pongamos punto final al asunto

“let's put a final point to the matter”

To bring a matter, argument, or disagreement to a definitive close and not return to it.

Ya hemos discutido esto suficiente. Pongamos punto final al asunto.

We've argued about this enough. Let's put it to rest.

Italiano

Mettiamoci una pietra sopra

“let's put a stone over it”

To consider a disagreement, quarrel, or misunderstanding settled and move past it.

È stato un malinteso stupido. Dai, mettiamoci una pietra sopra.

It was a silly misunderstanding. Come on, let's let it go.

El puente · Il ponte

Both close the matter, but with different objects. Italian's stone comes from the image of sealing a grave — final, physical, a little heavy. Spanish reaches for a punctuation mark, the mark that ends a piece of writing — administrative rather than physical. Same function, genuinely different weight.

ES → IT

Mettiamo un punto finale exists but reads as more bureaucratic/written than mettiamoci una pietra sopra, which is the warmer, spoken form for patching things up personally. Mettiamoci una pietra sopra implies forgiveness or burying the matter, not just ending discussion.

IT → ES

Pongamos una piedra encima isn't the Spanish idiom — a Spanish listener wouldn't reliably parse the stone image the way an Italian one would. Pongamos punto final al asunto is cooler and more administrative — it doesn't carry the emotional 'bury it' tone of the Italian.
