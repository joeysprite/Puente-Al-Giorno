Español

Morirse de hambre

“to die-oneself of hunger”

To be starving (figurative); the reflexive marks the hyperbole.

¿Ya vamos a comer? Me muero de hambre.

Are we eating soon? I'm starving.

Italiano

Morire di fame

“to die of hunger”

To be starving (figurative) — or literally to starve; context decides.

Quando mangiamo? Muoio di fame.

When are we eating? I'm starving.

El puente · Il ponte

Both die of hunger daily and survive — but Spanish has a switch Italian lacks: the reflexive. Morirse (with -se) is the hyperbole; plain morir leans literal. Italian's morire di fame does both jobs and lets context sort it out. The pattern generalizes across the whole family — me muero de risa / de sueño / de ganas — and Italian answers with a preposition twist of its own: morire DAL ridere, DALLA voglia (da + article for the cause), not always di.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Importing the reflexive gives 'mi muoio di fame' — not Italian; the verb stands alone: muoio di fame. And the family shifts preposition: morire DAL ridere, not 'di ridere'.

IT → ES

Dropping the -se gives 'muero de hambre' — grammatical but oddly grave, like an actual famine report. Everyday hunger wants me muero.
