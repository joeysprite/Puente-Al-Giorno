Español

No está el horno para bollos

“the oven isn't ready for buns”

The situation or mood isn't right for something — not the moment to ask, joke, or bring something up.

No le pidas el coche ahora, que no está el horno para bollos: acaba de perder el trabajo.

Don't ask him for the car right now — it's not the moment, he just lost his job.

Italiano

Non è aria

“it's not air”

Not the right moment or mood for something. Can be a neutral heads-up or, depending on tone, a sharper warning that someone is genuinely annoyed.

Non chiedergli niente adesso: non è aria.

Don't ask him for anything right now — it's not the moment.

El puente · Il ponte

Same function, fully different domain. Spanish reaches into the kitchen — the oven isn't at temperature, so don't expect buns. Italian reaches into the atmosphere — the air itself is wrong. Neither image transfers; a learner has to drop the picture entirely and just learn the phrase whole. Register also isn't a flat match: native discussion of 'non è aria' repeatedly describes it as carrying real edge or irritation depending on tone, sharper than a neutral 'not now' — worth a native check on whether 'no está el horno para bollos' carries the same optional bite.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Non è forno per panini or similar literal renderings mean nothing in Italian — the oven image doesn't exist on this side. Non è aria can carry irritation or tension depending on tone — sharper than the Spanish oven image.

IT → ES

No es aire wouldn't be understood as this idiom in Spanish at all. No está el horno para bollos is gentler; it doesn't carry the edge non è aria can have.
