Español

Poner toda la carne en el asador

“to put all the meat on the grill”

To go all in; to commit everything to one attempt. ADMIRING.

Puso toda la carne en el asador y produjo la película ella misma.

She went all in and produced the film herself.

Italiano

Mettere troppa carne al fuoco

“to put too much meat on the fire”

To take on too much at once; to overcommit. CRITICAL.

Hai messo troppa carne al fuoco: non riuscirai a finire niente.

You've taken on too much — you'll finish nothing.

El puente · Il ponte

The same meat on the same fire, and the two languages deliver opposite verdicts. Spanish admires it: all the meat on the grill, holding nothing back, the gambler's whole stake. Italian scolds it: TOO much meat on the fire, more than anyone could cook, ambition curdling into chaos. The word that decides the judgement is troppa — and a Spanish speaker who translates their own compliment has just called someone reckless.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

The costly one: praising someone with 'ha messo tutta la carne al fuoco' lands as criticism. For genuine all-in praise Italian says 'ha dato tutto se stesso'.

IT → ES

Translating the Italian warning into Spanish produces a compliment — 'poner toda la carne en el asador' is admiring. To warn, Spanish says 'abarcar demasiado'.
