Español

A hierro candente, batir de repente

“on red-hot iron, strike at once”

Act while the moment is hot; deal with it now.

Habla con él hoy que está receptivo: a hierro candente, batir de repente.

Talk to him today while he's open to it — strike while the iron's hot.

Italiano

Battere il ferro finché è caldo

“to beat the iron while it is hot”

Act while conditions favour you.

Parlagli adesso: bisogna battere il ferro finché è caldo.

Talk to him now — strike while the iron's hot.

El puente · Il ponte

The same forge, and this time Spanish is the one showing off: candente/repente rhymes, so the refrán arrives as a couplet, while Italian states the smithing plainly with a subordinate clause. The exact inversion of chi non risica non rosica, where the rhyme sits on the Italian side. Neither language rhymes on principle — each just got lucky with different proverbs.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

The Spanish rhyme cannot be carried over; Italian keeps the finché clause — battere il ferro finché è caldo.

IT → ES

Translating the Italian literally ('golpear el hierro mientras está caliente') is understood but is not the refrán, and loses the rhyme that makes it memorable.
