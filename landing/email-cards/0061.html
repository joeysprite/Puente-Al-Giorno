Español

A lo hecho, pecho

“to what is done, chest”

What's done is done — square your shoulders and face it.

Ya firmaste, así que a lo hecho, pecho.

You've signed it, so face the music.

Italiano

Piangere sul latte versato

“to cry over spilled milk”

To lament what can no longer be changed (usually in the negative: don't).

Inutile piangere sul latte versato: pensiamo a domani.

No use crying over spilled milk — let's think about tomorrow.

El puente · Il ponte

Same predicament, opposite grammar of advice. Italian names the useless behaviour and tells you to stop it — don't cry over the milk. Spanish names the required posture instead and tells you to adopt it: chest out to what's done. One proverb forbids, the other prescribes; the Spanish is four syllables and a rhyme, the Italian a full clause.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

There is no milk in the Spanish idiom; 'llorar sobre la leche derramada' is a calque from English that natives will understand and not use.

IT → ES

'A lo hecho, pecho' has no Italian equivalent as compressed — the nearest is a full sentence, not a motto.
