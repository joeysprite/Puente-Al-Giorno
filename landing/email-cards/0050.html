B2

Español

Tener dos dedos de frente

“to have two fingers of forehead”

To have common sense; to show basic good judgment. Usually heard in the negative.

Si tuviera dos dedos de frente, no habría firmado ese contrato.

If he had any sense, he wouldn't have signed that contract.

Italiano

Avere sale in zucca

“to have salt in the pumpkin”

To have good sense; to be sensible. Usually heard in the negative.

Se avesse un po' di sale in zucca, non firmerebbe quel contratto.

If he had any sense, he wouldn't sign that contract.

El puente · Il ponte

Both languages measure sense by a physical quantity in the head — and both mostly use it to say someone hasn't got any. Spanish measures the forehead in finger-widths; Italian measures salt in the pumpkin. Neither is about knowledge: you can have a doctorate and still, in both languages, be short two fingers or an empty pumpkin.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

'Due dita di fronte' is not the Italian idiom — the sense lives in the gourd: avere sale in zucca.

IT → ES

'Tener sal en la calabaza' means nothing in Spanish. The measure is the forehead: dos dedos de frente.
