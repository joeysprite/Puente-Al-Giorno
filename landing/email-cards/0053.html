Español

Hoy por ti, mañana por mí

“today for you, tomorrow for me”

I help you now, you help me later — reciprocity between equals.

No te preocupes, yo te cubro el turno: hoy por ti, mañana por mí.

Don't worry, I'll cover your shift — you'd do the same for me.

Italiano

Oggi a me, domani a te

“today to me, tomorrow to you”

What happens to me now will happen to you later — reciprocity, and often a warning.

Tocca a me oggi, ma oggi a me, domani a te.

It's my turn today — but your turn comes.

El puente · Il ponte

The two halves are swapped, and the mood swaps with them. Spanish starts with YOU — today's favor is mine to give, tomorrow's is yours: warm, transactional, between friends. Italian starts with ME, and the reciprocity it promises is often misfortune rather than help. Same structure, reversed perspective, opposite temperature.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Word-for-word 'oggi per te, domani per me' misses the fixed Italian order — and the fixed Italian carries a shadow the Spanish does not.

IT → ES

Reading the Spanish as ominous misfires; between colleagues it simply means I've got you covered.
