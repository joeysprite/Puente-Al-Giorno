Español

Tener buen diente

“to have a good tooth”

To have a hearty appetite; to be a big eater.

Tu hijo tiene buen diente, repitió tres veces.

Your son's a good eater — he went back three times.

Italiano

Essere una buona forchetta

“to be a good fork”

To love eating; to have a big appetite.

Tuo figlio è una buona forchetta, ha fatto il bis tre volte.

Your son loves his food — three helpings.

El puente · Il ponte

A compliment paid with two different objects: Spanish credits the TOOTH — the body doing the work — while Italian credits the FORK, the tool. And the grammar follows the metaphor: in Spanish you HAVE a good tooth (something you possess), in Italian you ARE a good fork (something you become at the table). Have versus be, body versus instrument, in six words.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

'Avere una buona forchetta' is the classic slip — Italian says ESSERE una buona forchetta. You are the fork.

IT → ES

'Ser un buen diente' misfires; Spanish keeps it possessive: TENER buen diente.
