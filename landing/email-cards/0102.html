Español

Agua pasada no mueve molino

“water that has passed moves no mill”

What's done is done; no use dwelling on the past.

Ya te pedí perdón: agua pasada no mueve molino.

I apologised — it's water under the bridge.

Italiano

Acqua passata non macina più

“water that has passed grinds no more”

What's done is done; the past is past.

Ti ho già chiesto scusa: acqua passata non macina più.

I apologised — that's water under the bridge.

El puente · Il ponte

The same proverb, the same mill, one verb apart — and the verb is the whole lesson. Spanish says the passed water no longer MOVES the mill (mover, to move the whole machine); Italian says it no longer GRINDS (macinare, the specific work a mill does). Both picture the stream that has already flowed past the wheel and cannot be called back. English drops the mill entirely for water under a bridge.

La trampa · La trappola

ES → IT

Italian names the mill's actual job: non MACINA più. 'Non muove il mulino' will be understood but is not the fixed proverb.

IT → ES

'Agua pasada no muele' is not the Spanish form; it moves the mill — no mueve molino.
